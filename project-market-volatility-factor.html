Market Volatility × Factor Robustness

A walk-forward test of whether volatility regimes change factor payoffs—and whether a simple, ex-ante risk budget reduces drawdown in a diversified factor sleeve after costs.

Author: Jingrou ZengSample: 2011-01-03 to 2026-06-30Python · pandas · reproducible pipeline

Executive finding

Factor behavior is state-dependent, but not every apparent regime gap is statistically robust. The largest Sharpe deterioration from low to high volatility occurs in RMW. A pre-specified de-risking rule lowers annualized volatility from 4.6% to 2.8% and changes maximum drawdown from -12.8% to -6.3%, after a 2 bp turnover cost. The cost is lower annualized return and Sharpe; the result supports a risk-control use case, not an alpha claim.

1. Observation → hypothesis

Market stress does more than raise total volatility. Funding constraints, deleveraging, crowded positioning and changes in investor risk tolerance can alter the return distribution of long-short equity factors. The testable hypothesis is therefore:

H1: conditional factor Sharpe ratios differ across volatility states. H2: reducing aggregate factor exposure in high-volatility states can improve drawdown control without using same-day information.

2. Methodology and anti-leakage rules

  1. Inputs: daily Fama-French five factors plus momentum; Cboe VIX close.
  2. Ex-ante state: day t uses VIX observed on t-1. Low/Mid/High cutoffs are rolling 33rd/67th percentiles computed over the preceding 756 trading days, with a 252-day warm-up.
  3. Descriptive test: compare annualized return, volatility and Sharpe by state.
  4. Uncertainty: estimate 95% intervals for High-minus-Low annualized mean returns with 2,000 moving-block bootstrap draws (20-day blocks).
  5. Execution rule: start from an equal-weight six-factor sleeve; de-risk toward 5% ex-ante volatility using trailing 63-day data; apply risk budgets of 1.00/0.75/0.50 in Low/Mid/High regimes; never lever above 1.00; charge 2 bp per unit of exposure turnover.
  6. Validation: report full sample, 2010-2019, 2020-latest and VIX≥30 stress days separately.

6. Interpretation and decision rules

  • Use the regime as a risk control, not a return oracle. Volatility states are more reliable for scaling exposure than for claiming a factor will have a positive return.
  • Preserve falsifiability. A factor-state relationship is promoted only when its interval excludes zero and its sign is stable across subperiods.
  • Separate research from deployment. A production version would need investable factor portfolios, financing/borrow costs, market impact, capacity and region-specific validation.
  • Monitor drift. Re-estimate only on a scheduled cadence and compare new results with the frozen baseline to avoid threshold chasing.

7. Limitations

Fama-French factors are research portfolios, not directly tradeable products. VIX is a US equity volatility proxy and may partly reflect information also embedded in factor returns. Results are conditional associations, not causal estimates. The sample ends with the latest common observation in the downloaded files (2026-06-30); the factor archive itself is based on the June 2026 CRSP vintage.

Reproducibility

Run python src/analyze.py from the project folder. The script reads the raw official-source files, rebuilds the processed dataset, tables, figures and this report. Random resampling uses a fixed seed.
